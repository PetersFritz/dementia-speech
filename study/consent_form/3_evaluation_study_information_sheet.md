# Participant Information Sheet

## Evaluation study

**Project title:** [Project title]

You are being invited to take part in a research study. Before you decide, please read the following information carefully so you understand why the research is being done and what taking part would involve. Please contact us if anything is unclear.

### What is the study about?

We have developed a clinician-facing "decision support dashboard" that presents speech-based features from established cognitive assessment tasks (verbal fluency and the "Cookie Theft" picture description) in an interpretable visual form. The dashboard is designed to support clinical reasoning about whether a case reflects healthy cognition, mild cognitive impairment (MCI), or dementia. It does not itself give a diagnosis; it presents information for you to interpret. This study evaluates how clinicians use the dashboard to reason about cases, and compares clinicians' judgements with those of automated systems.

### Why have I been invited?

You have been invited because you are a clinician or healthcare professional involved in, or familiar with, the assessment of dementia. The core group we are inviting is specialists with direct diagnostic experience. Resources permitting, we are also extending recruitment to generalists such as GPs and nurse practitioners, to see how clinicians less routinely involved in this specific diagnostic task engage with the dashboard. You have not taken part in the earlier design or feedback stages of this project. [If applicable: keep this last sentence; those who took part in co-design/feedback are not eligible.]

### Do I have to take part?

No. Taking part is entirely voluntary, and you may stop at any time. There is no obligation to take part, and no inducement or payment is offered.

### What will happen if I take part?

Taking part happens in two steps. First, you read this information sheet and give your consent online, by ticking a short set of statements on this form; no data about the study tasks is collected at this point, and no Google account or sign-in is needed to do this. As soon as you submit your consent, we automatically email you a personal, secure link to the study itself, together with a copy of this information sheet and a record of the consent responses you just gave, for your own records.

Second, you follow that personal link to complete the study, online, at a time that suits you. You do not need to attend a scheduled session, and you can close the page and return to the same link later to continue where you left off. Taking part involves:

- Answering a few short questions about yourself as a professional: your role, your care setting, your years of clinical experience, and how often you are involved in dementia assessment. These questions are a required part of taking part.
- A short set of instructions and a practice case, to familiarise you with the dashboard.
- Reviewing a set of **40 de-identified cases**. For each case, the dashboard shows speech features across three views (an overview, a verbal-fluency view, and a picture-description view). For each case you record your judgement (healthy / MCI / dementia) and how confident you are. The time taken for each case is recorded automatically.
- A short closing questionnaire about your experience of using the dashboard (a mix of rating-scale and free-text questions).

The cases you review contain no patient-identifiable information.

### What are the possible disadvantages or risks of taking part?

We do not anticipate any significant risks. The main cost to you is your time. This is an evaluation of the dashboard, not of you: you will not receive feedback on whether your judgements were correct, your individual performance is never identified or reported, and results are only ever reported in aggregate.

### What are the possible benefits of taking part?

There is no direct personal benefit, but your participation will help evaluate a tool intended to support the assessment of cognitive decline.

### What data is collected, and how is it kept confidential?

Your responses (your judgements, confidence ratings, and decision times, along with your questionnaire answers) are recorded under an anonymous participant code. This is stored directly on a University-managed server, not on any third-party or cloud service, and is accessible only to the research team. The email address used to send you your study link is held separately from your responses and is not stored alongside them, so your responses are not linked to your identity in the research dataset.

The data you provide is collected in order to be analysed as part of this research, and the findings will be written up and published in academic outputs (such as journal articles, conference papers, and theses). This includes the short professional questions you answer about yourself, which are used to describe the participant group and to compare how different groups of clinicians use the dashboard. This professional information is collected as fixed-choice categories, not free text, and is reported only in aggregate, so you cannot be identified from it. Your anonymised data may also be shared with other researchers in the CognoSpeak group for research purposes connected to this project, who will use it only for those purposes. You will not be identifiable in any report, publication, or shared dataset.

Your email address, which we hold only to send you your personal study link, is kept separately from your responses and is destroyed within six months of the end of the project (expected 17 September 2027, giving a destruction date of 17 March 2028). Your anonymised responses are not personal data once your email address is removed, and may be retained and archived beyond that point for verification, reuse and publication purposes.

The University of Sheffield is the Data Controller for this project. The University processes personal data for research in the public interest.
