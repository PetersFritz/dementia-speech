## Tool 3: Closing questionnaire (evaluation study) — DRAFT

Presented once, after the participant has completed all cases. Comprises a set of rating-scale items and a small number of free-text prompts covering the participant's experience of the dashboard. Marked as draft; item wording may be refined following the co-design phase.

**Rating-scale items.** Each rated on a 5-point scale from "Strongly disagree" (1) to "Strongly agree" (5).

1. The dashboard was easy to use.
2. The information presented was clear and easy to understand.
3. The dashboard presented information that was useful for making a judgement.
4. I could find the information I needed without difficulty.
5. The three views (overview, verbal fluency, picture description) worked well together.
6. I felt confident using the dashboard to reason about cases.
7. I would trust information presented in this way to support my clinical reasoning.
8. Using the dashboard felt mentally demanding. *(reverse-scored)*
9. I would be willing to use a tool like this in practice.

**Free-text prompts.**

10. What did you find most useful about the dashboard?
11. What did you find confusing, unclear, or unhelpful, if anything?
12. Is there anything you would add, remove, or change?
13. Any other comments?

## Tool 4: Focus group and feedback topic guide (co-design and feedback phases) — INDICATIVE / DRAFT

An indicative guide to the topics and example prompts used in the co-design focus groups (Phase 1) and the feedback session (Phase 2). These sessions are semi-structured and discussion-based; the facilitator will use these topics flexibly, following the direction of the conversation and adapting or omitting prompts as appropriate. This is not a fixed script.

**Introduction and context**
- Brief overview of the project and the purpose of the session.
- Participants' own experience of assessing cognition and dementia, and of speech-based assessment.

**Reactions to the dashboard (walkthrough)**
- First impressions on seeing the dashboard.
- Example prompts: What stands out first? Is it clear what you are looking at? What would you expect to be able to do here?

**Content and usefulness**
- Which pieces of information seem most and least useful for reasoning about a case?
- Is anything missing that you would want to see?
- Example prompts: Would this information change how you think about a case? What would you trust, and what would you want to check?

**Clarity and presentation**
- Are the visualisations and labels clear and understandable?
- Is anything ambiguous, misleading, or easy to misread?
- Example prompts: How would you describe this view to a colleague? Is anything harder to interpret than it should be?

**Structure and layout**
- Does the organisation across the three views (overview, verbal fluency, picture description) make sense?
- Is the flow between views intuitive?

**Fit with practice**
- How well would a tool like this fit into how you work?
- What would help or hinder its use in practice?

**Feedback session (Phase 2) additional focus**
- Whether earlier design input has been addressed in the full draft.
- Any remaining issues to resolve before the dashboard is finalised.

**Closing**
- Overall impressions and any final comments.
